# Session 13 — Midterm Review · outline for approval

**Monday October 12 · 75 min · Objectives 1, 2, 3, 4 · M4 due tonight, 11:59 PM**

Students were told to bring questions and their computers. The midterm is
Wednesday October 21, in class, 75 minutes, closed resource. It covers
sessions 3 to 12, through Wednesday October 7. UML is taught on Wednesday
October 14 but is not on the exam.

The review teaches no new concept. It walks the topic list and has students do
practice problems in the exam's two formats: short questions and writing a small class. The exam tests concepts with
small, general examples, not the chess engine, and so does the practice. No
practice problem repeats a midterm question. The topic list and problems are
in `study-guide.md`; the answers are in `notes-INSTRUCTOR-ONLY.md`.

## Logistics (0–5 min)

What the exam looks like: four parts and their points, closed resource, what
to bring, what is and is not covered. Session 2's tooling, the git loop, and
UML are not on it. DPRC letters by Wednesday.

## The topic list (5–15 min)

One slide per group: modeling; composition and inheritance; collections,
generics, exceptions; design quality; refactoring; information hiding. For
each concept the room gives a one-line example before the slide shows one.

## Practice (15–70 min)

Three rounds, each 7 minutes of pair work and 5 to 7 of discussion. Students
work on paper, as on the exam.

- **Round 1 (15–28).** Practice 1–8: buckets, validation,
  `abstract` and `protected`, override and overload, interface and
  implementation, removing while iterating, SOLID, refactoring or not.
- **Round 2 (28–43).** Practice 9–11 and 16: choosing a
  collection, saying no, a leak, dispatch.
- **Round 3 (43–58).** Practice 12–15: smells, has-a or is-a, interface or
  abstract class, separation of concerns.
- **Coding (58–70).** Practice 17, a `Song` record and a `Playlist` class,
  written alone on paper; pairs then swap and mark for compile errors.

## Open questions and M4 (70–75 min)

Questions from the room on any session. M4 questions answered from the
handout and the tests, without writing `MoveGenerator`'s bodies on the
projector.

## What this session does not do

- No new concept and no new code on the projector.
- No `MoveGenerator` bodies, nothing from M5.
- No question that appears on the midterm, in any form a student could
  memorise.
